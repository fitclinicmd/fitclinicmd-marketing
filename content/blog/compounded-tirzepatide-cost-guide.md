## What Is Compounded Tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide is the active ingredient in the FDA-approved medicines Mounjaro and Zepbound. A compounded version is prepared by a pharmacy based on a prescription. It is not an FDA-approved generic or automatically interchangeable with either brand-name medicine. Understanding that distinction can help you ask more informed questions about a treatment’s price, quality, and availability.

### Why compounded tirzepatide isn’t an FDA-approved generic

Generic drugs must go through FDA review before they can be marketed as generics. Compounded medications do not go through that same approval process. The FDA does not review a compounded tirzepatide product for safety, effectiveness, or quality before it is made available to patients.

That does not mean every compounded medication is prepared improperly. It does mean you should not assume it has been evaluated or confirmed as equivalent to Mounjaro or Zepbound. A lower price alone does not establish that the product is interchangeable with an FDA-approved medicine. [Medical News Today explains why compounded tirzepatide is not an FDA-approved generic](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/drugs-compounded-tirzepatide-vs-mounjaro).

### How compounded medication differs from FDA-approved tirzepatide

A compounded product may differ from an FDA-approved medicine in its formulation, concentration, dosage form, or instructions. Its preparation and quality can also depend on the pharmacy making it. Because compounded tirzepatide has not undergone the same FDA review as Mounjaro or Zepbound, you should not assume that the products have been shown to work the same way or carry the same quality assurances.

The differences matter when measuring and taking a dose. Do not transfer dosing instructions or injection techniques from one product to another. Ask the prescriber or pharmacist to explain the exact concentration, dose, storage requirements, and measuring method for the medication you receive. GoodRx outlines important considerations.

### How federal rules and product availability can change

Federal rules limit when pharmacies can compound copies of FDA-approved drugs. The FDA determined that the tirzepatide shortage was resolved, which changed the conditions under which pharmacies could compound copies under shortage-related provisions. Limited exceptions may apply, including certain patient-specific situations, but they do not mean that compounded tirzepatide is broadly available or appropriate for everyone.

Rules, court decisions, and FDA policies can change, so a clinic’s past ability to offer a medication does not guarantee it can offer it now. Before paying for treatment, ask the prescriber what applies to your situation and check the [FDA’s current compounding guidance](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/compounding-and-fda-questions-and-answers) and [drug shortage updates](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/drug-shortages).
