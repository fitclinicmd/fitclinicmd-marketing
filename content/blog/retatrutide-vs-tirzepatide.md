## Key Takeaways

* **Status is the first difference.** Tirzepatide has FDA-approved uses. Retatrutide does not, and Lilly says it cannot be sold to consumers.
* **They are not the same kind of medicine.** Tirzepatide acts on two hormone receptors. Retatrutide is being studied as a triple agonist that also acts on glucagon receptors.
* **Trial numbers are not interchangeable.** Published averages come from different studies, doses, and time frames. They do not forecast one person's result.
* **Buying a product labeled retatrutide is not a shortcut.** Lilly warns that products sold as retatrutide outside its trials are unlawful and may be unsafe.

## How Each Medicine Is Built

Both names show up in weight-loss conversations because they act on gut-hormone signals that affect appetite and blood sugar. They do not act on the same set of receptors, and that design difference is not the same thing as a better medicine for you.

### Tirzepatide acts on two receptors

Tirzepatide is a lab-made molecule that acts on two hormones released after eating: GIP and GLP-1. The approved products are once-weekly injections. Zepbound is the weight-management brand. Mounjaro is the type 2 diabetes brand. Sharing an ingredient does not make those brands the same. Our [GLP-1 overview](https://www.fitclinicmd.com/glp-1) explains this two-hormone action next to semaglutide, which uses one pathway. That is a different comparison from this page.

### Retatrutide adds a third receptor

Lilly describes retatrutide as a once-weekly study drug that acts on GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors. Glucagon is a third hormone signal. Researchers are studying whether that extra signal changes weight and other health measures. A third receptor is a description of the drug. It is not proof that retatrutide is safer, stronger, or right for any person. Lilly is still studying safety and benefit in its own trials.

## What the Trials Actually Measured

Search results often place one percentage next to the other. Those percentages answer different questions. Read the population, the dose, the length of the study, and whether the drug was already approved when the number was published.

### Tirzepatide has an approved-drug evidence base

[SURMOUNT-5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40353578/) was a phase 3b trial in adults with obesity and without type 2 diabetes. At 72 weeks, people assigned to tirzepatide (10 mg or 15 mg) lost an average of 20.2% of body weight. People assigned to semaglutide (1.7 mg or 2.4 mg) lost an average of 13.7%. Waist circumference fell by an average of 18.4 cm with tirzepatide and 13.0 cm with semaglutide. Most side effects were gastrointestinal, mild or moderate, and tied to dose increases. That trial compared two approved medicines. It says nothing about retatrutide.

[FDA labeling](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s041lbl.pdf), not a blog summary, is the source for who may be a candidate. Zepbound's weight-management use is for adults with obesity, or adults who are overweight with a weight-related condition. It is meant to be used with a reduced-calorie diet and more activity. The same active ingredient, in Mounjaro, is approved for type 2 diabetes.

A separate Zepbound use covers moderate to severe sleep apnea in adults with obesity. Labels also carry a boxed warning about thyroid tumors seen in rats. People with a personal or family history of a rare thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma should not use it. The same is true for people with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2.

### Retatrutide results are still investigational

On May 21, 2026, Lilly reported topline results from TRIUMPH-1 (NCT05929066). The [peer-reviewed phase 3 report](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2604169) was published September 29, 2026. It was an 80-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial that enrolled 2,339 adults with obesity or overweight, at least one weight-related condition, and no diabetes. Participants were assigned to retatrutide 4 mg, 9 mg, 12 mg, or placebo. Everyone on retatrutide started at 2 mg once weekly and stepped up every four weeks.

Lilly's topline report described 19.0% (47.2 lb) average loss at 4 mg, 25.9% (64.4 lb) at 9 mg, and 28.3% (70.3 lb) at 12 mg. The later peer-reviewed report used a treatment-regimen estimand and reported mean changes of 17.6%, 23.7%, and 25.0%, respectively, versus 3.9% with placebo. These are different analyses of the same trial, not a head-to-head comparison with tirzepatide.

A planned extension kept some people with a starting BMI of 35 or higher on treatment through 104 weeks. In that extension, the 12 mg group averaged 30.3% loss (85.0 lb). Lilly also reported changes in waist size, non-HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, blood pressure, and a blood marker of inflammation called hsCRP.

Lilly's topline results were released before the full journal paper was public, so the two reports use different analysis conventions. The peer-reviewed result is the current source for the trial's reported treatment-regimen estimates. Neither report turns a study drug into an approved one.

Common adverse events in TRIUMPH-1 matched other incretin trials: nausea, diarrhea, constipation, and vomiting, and they were more common at higher doses. At 12 mg, nausea was reported in 42.4% of participants versus 14.8% on placebo. Discontinuation for adverse events was 4.1% at 4 mg, 6.9% at 9 mg, and 11.3% at 12 mg, versus 4.9% on placebo. Dysesthesia, an abnormal skin sensation, occurred in 12.5% at 12 mg versus 0.9% on placebo. Most of those events were mild or moderate, and many resolved during treatment. These are study observations, not a side-effect list you can use at home.

## Why the Percentages Do Not Pick a Winner

Putting 20.2% next to 25.0% feels like a ranking. It is not. The tirzepatide number comes from a 72-week study against semaglutide in adults without diabetes. The retatrutide numbers come from an 80-week study against a placebo, with different doses, analysis conventions, and a different step-up plan. The people, the time frames, and the comparison drugs do not match.

Averages also hide the spread. Some participants lose less than the mean, some lose more, and some stop because of side effects. Dose, other illnesses, other medicines, and whether someone can stay on treatment all change the outcome. No study result is a personal forecast, and no headline is a reason to seek an unapproved drug.

There is a second trap. Results from approved-brand trials do not carry over to compounded or copycat products. The [FDA has warned about unapproved GLP-1 products](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss) that were not reviewed for safety, benefit, or quality. FitClinicMD's [FDA status guide](https://www.fitclinicmd.com/peptides/fda-status) separates approved medicines from compounded ones. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. A compounded product is not retatrutide just because a seller uses that word.

## What You Can and Cannot Get Today

Tirzepatide may be prescribed as an FDA-approved product when a licensed clinician decides it fits that person's history, goals, and the product's labeling. In some circumstances, a clinician may discuss a compounded formulation where lawful and clinically appropriate, but compounded medicines are not FDA-approved and are not interchangeable with the approved brands. A prescription is a clinical decision. It is not something a website, a quiz, or a comparison article can grant.

Retatrutide cannot. [Lilly says](https://medical.lilly.com/us/products/answers/is-retatrutide-available-in-the-consumer-market-381272) retatrutide is still investigational and is not available for purchase by consumers. The company says it owns the rights to retatrutide and warns against products sold outside its trials. A legal conclusion about compounding depends on applicable law and the facts of a specific product, so readers should not treat a seller's label as proof of safety, quality, or lawful availability.

Lilly has said it plans to submit a biologics license application in the first quarter of 2027, after a full manufacturing-data package. There is no guarantee of approval or of that timing. Until the FDA approves a product and a prescriber finds it appropriate, retatrutide is a trial drug, not a care option.

If you did not receive a drug inside a Lilly-sponsored trial, do not assume a vial, pen, or powder labeled retatrutide is the study medicine. Report suspicious products rather than using them. ## Questions Worth Asking a Clinician

A useful visit is about your history, not about chasing the largest percentage in a press release. These questions keep the conversation on care a clinician can actually consider:

1. Which of my conditions, medicines, and prior side effects matter before any weight-management drug is discussed?
2. If an approved option is on the table, which brand's labeling applies, and what is it approved to treat?
3. What results were seen in studies of people similar to me, and what can those averages not predict?
4. Which side effects should I report quickly, including severe stomach symptoms or signs of an allergic reaction?
5. How will labs, follow-up, and a plan for stopping or changing treatment work if I start something?
6. How should I respond if a seller offers an unapproved drug by name, including retatrutide?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is retatrutide the same as tirzepatide?

No. Tirzepatide acts on GIP and GLP-1 receptors. It is the ingredient in FDA-approved Mounjaro and Zepbound. Retatrutide is a study drug that also acts on glucagon receptors. It is not FDA-approved, and it is not another brand name for tirzepatide.

### Is retatrutide FDA-approved?

No. As of Lilly's public statements through 2026, retatrutide remains in phase 3 trials and cannot be sold to consumers. Lilly has said it plans to submit an application in the first quarter of 2027. A plan to file is not an approval, and the FDA has not authorized it for any use.

### Which produces more weight loss, retatrutide or tirzepatide?

There is no head-to-head trial that answers that for an individual. SURMOUNT-5 reported 20.2% average loss with tirzepatide at 72 weeks. The peer-reviewed TRIUMPH-1 report found a 25.0% mean change with retatrutide 12 mg at 80 weeks under its treatment-regimen estimand, while Lilly's earlier topline report said 28.3% under a different analysis. Different designs, doses, estimands, and populations mean those figures cannot be ranked against each other or applied to you.

### Can a clinic prescribe compounded retatrutide?

No. Lilly warns that retatrutide is not available for consumer purchase and that products sold outside its trials may be unsafe. A compounding pharmacy cannot turn a study drug into an FDA-approved treatment. Be wary of any site that offers retatrutide for sale, and ask a licensed clinician or pharmacist about any product you are considering.
